This repair removes per-receipt synchronous persistence from the persistent RPC accounting path. Financial SQLite durability, mandatory trading evidence, request starts, physical reservations, invocation identity, provider limits and physical settlement are unchanged.

The actual `ComponentBudget` reproduced a 12.400-second delay of a 50 ms timer when 1,200 passive 4 KiB receipts each experienced an explicitly injected 10 ms durability latency. Zero HTTP requests were made. This proves a local blocking mechanism; it does not establish that the retained live 12.329-second breach had this cause.

Persistent counterbook schema 2 uses a pre-durable passive reservation of 4 MiB: the existing maximum accepted 2 MiB receipt plus a 2 MiB coalescing window. It exists before transport receipt, and is replenished after a receipt when less than 2 MiB remains. Thus even a replenishment failure leaves the entire just-received maximum-size payload covered by the preceding durable book. Exact accepted bytes consume that reservation in memory. Replenishment durably checkpoints the exact observed prefix and reserves the next bounded tail before another receipt can be accepted. A trailing one-second checkpoint coalesces receipts; late timers do not weaken the already-durable bound. Graceful close flushes exact bytes and clears unused future capacity. A crash retains the bounded tail (at most 4 MiB) as uncertainty, separately from measured bytes, with zero invented request starts or physical operations. Reopening never refunds or repeatedly charges the same historical uncertainty.

HTTP bodies already have a durable 2 MiB physical-operation reservation. Persistent chunk receipts update exact in-memory counts inside that bound; invocation/settlement and other ordinary durable checkpoints flush the measured prefix before releasing its reservation. Incomplete operations retain the existing conservative physical uncertainty.

Legacy finite-research books keep schema 1 and their original immediate receipt persistence. Historical sources are read-only. The updated baseline/checkpoint readers understand schema 2 and retain its passive byte uncertainty across compaction and restart. Older executables must refuse these new counterbooks rather than silently ignore the new field; rollback must use the new reader even when selecting the baseline strategy.

The wider critical path still includes mandatory evidence fsyncs, synchronous financial transactions, report/checkpoint fsyncs, and compound candidate SDK work. This change is one coordinated component of that repair, not a claim that all possible protection stalls are eliminated or that forward acceptance has passed.

Verified failure boundaries

- Passive capacity is pre-funded in the constructor, not during the first already-received message. The 2 MiB safety tail plus 2 MiB batching window covers an entire maximum-size receipt if replenishment fails.
- A later in-flight HTTP settlement can durably record a partial passive tail after a transient refill failure. Readers retain any valid 0–4 MiB tail as uncertainty; they do not reject a truthful partial tail merely because normal pre-acceptance headroom is larger.
- A passive accounting-epoch rollover consumes its current already-received payload in the OLD durable reservation before closing that book. Failed creation of the next epoch cannot erase the current receipt and is contained as a storage failure.
- An active book's unreceived future capacity may be released at verified graceful close. Tests require all observed bytes, physical pending uncertainty, request starts, provider history and original timestamps to remain unchanged; a closed historical book stays immutable.

Other synchronous critical-path activities remain: essential recorder writes perform active-file fsync plus state-file/directory durability; financial transactions use FULL WAL durability; report and watcher checkpoints write multiple atomic files. Proof bulk copying and hash verification are asynchronous, with synchronous sealing/publication boundaries. Backup artifact inspection/normalization/pruning already run in a worker. These mechanisms must be considered alongside the separate candidate compound-operation and audit scheduling repairs.
